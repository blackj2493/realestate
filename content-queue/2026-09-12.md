- **Figure (verbatim):** `73 days`
- **Region:** Oakville
- **Sample:** 1,108 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why surprising:** Longest median *true* days-on-market of the markets tracked
  today — and it belongs to one of the GTA's most expensive towns, not a soft
  outer-ring market.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Oakville has the slowest market of any GTA town we track right now — median true DOM is 73 days

**Body:**
```
Pulled the median true days-on-market across 15 Ontario markets this morning. Oakville is the slowest of the lot: the typical active listing has now sat 73 days. Sample is 1,108 active listings.

"True" DOM is the part worth flagging. When a listing is cancelled and relisted, the board's days-on-market clock resets to zero — so the headline number you see on the consumer portals can look weeks fresher than the property actually is. This counts from the original list, so a relist doesn't wipe the history. TRREB and the boards have the underlying dates; they just don't surface the un-reset version.

I don't have a tidy story for why Oakville specifically. Higher price bands sitting longer as rates bite is the obvious guess, but that's a guess, not what the number says. The number just says: 73 days, longest of the group.

Disclosure: I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (updated nightly): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

Tell me where I'm wrong — is there something about Oakville's mix (price band, detached share, new builds) that would make true DOM misleading here? AMA on the method.

## Angle 2 — Whitby: 3.2 months of supply

- **Figure (verbatim):** `3.2 months of supply`
- **Region:** Whitby
- **Sample:** 458 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature
- **Why surprising:** Least inventory of the 15 markets tracked today. Roughly 4–6
  months is balanced; below that tilts hard toward sellers — so while much of the
  region reads as cooling, Whitby is the tightest, most seller-favoured market on the
  board.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** While the region reads "cooling," Whitby is the tightest market we track — 3.2 months of supply

**Body:**
```
Ran months-of-supply across 15 Ontario markets. Whitby is the tightest of the group at 3.2 months of supply, off 458 active listings.

The rule of thumb most desks use: ~4–6 months of supply is a balanced market; below that tilts toward sellers, above it toward buyers. So 3.2 puts Whitby on the seller-favoured side while a lot of the surrounding coverage is about price cuts and slowing sales. Both can be true at once — supply is a stock measure, price cuts are a flow measure, and they don't have to move together in the same month.

TRREB and CREA publish months-of-supply too; the difference here is per-market and ranked side by side against 14 others, refreshed nightly, so you can see who's actually tight rather than reading a board-wide average.

Disclosure: I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker: https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature

Where I might be wrong: 458 listings is a real sample but Whitby is small enough that a slow sales month could swing the ratio. Anyone closer to Durham seeing this on the ground? AMA on how the ratio's built.
```

- **Why surprising:** Best gross rental yield of the markets tracked today (1-bed) —

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Best 1-bed gross yield of the 15 markets we track is Oshawa, ~6.3%

**Body:**
```
Compared gross rental yield on 1-beds across 15 Ontario markets. Oshawa comes out on top at about 6.3% gross yield, off a sample of 735.

Two things to keep honest about the number:
- It's *gross*. It's the annual rent as a share of price, before property tax, condo fees, maintenance, vacancy, or financing. Net will be a good bit lower, and financing at current rates is what actually decides cashflow.
- It's a market-level median for 1-beds, not any single unit. Your building, your maintenance fees, your tenant will vary.

What it's useful for is a first-pass screen: which markets clear a yield bar before you go unit-hunting, rather than eyeballing one listing at a time. The boards hold rent and sold data; stitching them into a per-market yield is the part they don't publish.

Disclosure: I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker: https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/rent-vs-buy

Tell me where I'm wrong — for the Durham investors here, does ~6.3% gross survive contact with Oshawa condo fees and taxes? AMA on how the yield's computed.
