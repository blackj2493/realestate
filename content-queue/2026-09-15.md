That's the highest cut-share of the 15 Ontario markets I track today. What I like about a

cut, per region, refreshed nightly. Same raw feed, a different question asked of it.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Oakville listing has now sat 75 days (median true DOM, n=1,053)

**Body:**

Oakville is the slowest-moving board I track today: the **median active listing has now sat
75 days**. Sample is 1,053 active listings.

The word doing the work there is *true*. On most portals days-on-market resets to zero when
a seller cancels and relists, which is why a home you've watched for months can show "6 days"
on a listing page. I stitch the history back together so the clock keeps running across
relists — so 75 days is closer to how long the property has actually been trying to sell.

Prior art, to be fair: HouseSigma surfaces relist history on a property if you dig, and
TRREB reports its own DOM monthly at the board level. The difference here is that the count
is the *median across active Oakville inventory*, computed the same way every night, rather
than per-listing or a monthly board average.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (updated nightly): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

Tell me where I'm wrong — does a 75-day median read as genuinely stale Oakville inventory to
you, or is that normal for the price band up there?
