### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Nearly a third of active Brampton listings have already cut their asking price (31%, n=2,032)

**Body:**

Pulled the Brampton numbers this morning and it's the softest board on the list I track:
**31% of active Brampton listings have cut their asking price**, with a typical reduction of
7.8%. Sample is 2,032 active listings, so this isn't a thin-data artifact.

That's the highest price-cut share of the 15 Ontario markets I look at today. The thing that
makes it easy to miss is that a "share of listings cut" doesn't show up in a headline average
price — a home relisted lower still prints as one active listing at its new number, so the
*share of sellers who've already blinked* gets averaged into nothing.

TRREB reports average and benchmark prices monthly, and HouseSigma shows the price history on
each individual property. What I'm measuring is different: the share of *active* Brampton
inventory that has taken at least one cut, refreshed nightly, per region. Not better than
those — just the angle the board-level averages hide.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (updated nightly): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/price-cuts

AMA, or tell me where I'm reading this wrong — is a 31% cut-share in Brampton real distress,
or just a lot of sellers who anchored high going into a slow month?

## Angle 2 — Ajax inventory (months of supply)

- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature
- **Why surprising:** Least inventory of the 15 markets tracked today — ≈4–6 months is
  balanced, and below that tilts hard toward sellers.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** While Brampton craters, Ajax is quietly the tightest market on my board — 2.9 months of supply (n=300)

**Body:**

Two markets, same morning, opposite stories. On the same board where Brampton is showing a
31% price-cut share, **Ajax is sitting at 2.9 months of supply** — the least inventory of the
15 Ontario markets I track today. Roughly 4–6 months is considered balanced; below that tilts
hard toward sellers. Sample is 300 active Ajax listings.

The reason I like months-of-supply over "is it a buyer's or seller's market" takes is that
it's just inventory divided by absorption — no vibes. And it's a useful corrective to the
blanket "everything in the GTA is softening" narrative: the softening is real in some boards
and absent in others, sometimes 30 minutes apart.

TRREB publishes months-of-supply at the board and municipality level in its monthly reports;
what I'm doing is the same metric refreshed nightly and lined up across 15 markets so you can
see the spread, not just one number.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (updated nightly): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature

AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — is 2.9 months in Ajax genuine tightness, or a low-listing
seasonal quirk that unwinds in the fall?

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Best 1-bed gross yield on my board right now is Oshawa at ~6.3% (n=716)

**Body:**

For the cashflow-minded: **a 1-bed in Oshawa grosses about 6.3% rental yield** right now —
the best gross rental yield of the markets I track today, on a 1-bed. Sample is 716.

Worth being precise about what that is and isn't: this is *gross* yield — annual rent over
price, before carrying costs, vacancy, condo fees, or tax. It's a top-of-funnel screen, not a
net-return number; the point is which markets are even worth underwriting, not a promise of
what lands in your pocket.

Rentals.ca and the CMHC rental reports publish market rents; what I'm doing is pairing rent
against current asking prices per region and showing the gross yield spread, refreshed nightly,
so the screening step is one look instead of two spreadsheets.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (updated nightly): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/rent-vs-buy
