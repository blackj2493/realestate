**Body:**

I've been pulling the aggregate price-cut share across the GTA markets I track, and Brampton is the outlier right now: **20% of active listings have cut their asking price**, and the typical cut is about 8.2%. That's the highest price-cut share of the 15 markets I'm watching today (n=2,382).

The number worth chewing on isn't the 20% — it's what it says about seller psychology. A cut isn't a sale. It's a seller who mispriced, watched the market not show up, and blinked first. When a fifth of a market is doing that at once, list price stops being a signal and starts being a starting bid. I stopped trusting the "reduced!" badge on the consumer portals a while ago — it tells you a number changed, not how far or how many times. So I started tracking the share and depth of cuts by market myself. ## Angle 2 — Richmond Hill: the typical active listing has sat 104 days

- **Figure (verbatim):** `104 days` (n=1,186)
- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why it's surprising:** Longest median true days-on-market of the markets tracked today.
- **Disclosure line:** *I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty, a free Ontario housing-data tracker.*

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Richmond Hill listing has now sat 104 days

**Body:**

Median **true days-on-market for active Richmond Hill listings is 104 days** — the longest of any market I track today (n=1,186). Not average dragged up by a few stale outliers; the median. Half the standing inventory has been on market longer than three months.

The reason this matters more than the headline: the consumer portals reset DOM every time a listing is cancelled and re-fired, so a place that's been shopped since spring reads as "listed 6 days ago." True DOM — the clock that doesn't reset on a relist — is the single most useful number for a buyer, because it's a direct proxy for seller fatigue. A 104-day median means a lot of Richmond Hill sellers are well past the stage where they're holding firm on price.

If you're buying here, sort by true DOM and start your conversations at the top of that list. The seller who's been carrying since May is a very different negotiation than the one who listed last week.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty, a free Ontario housing-data tracker; I track true (un-reset) DOM by market here → https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market. The whole reason the tracker exists is that the board feed makes the reset-DOM number the default one you see.

## Angle 3 — Ottawa: a 1-bed grosses about 7.2% rental yield

- **Figure (verbatim):** `7.2% gross yield` (1-bed; n=4,134)
- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/rent-vs-buy
- **Why it's surprising:** Best gross rental yield of the markets tracked today (1-bed).
- **Disclosure line:** *I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty, a free Ontario housing-data tracker.*

### (a) Reddit — r/ottawa

**Title:** Ottawa 1-beds are penciling the best gross rental yield of any market I track — about 7.2%

**Body:**

Running rent-vs-buy across the Ontario markets I track, **Ottawa 1-beds are grossing about 7.2% gross yield** — the best of any market today (n=4,134). That's a gross number (rent over price, before costs), not a cashflow promise — carrying costs, condo fees and vacancy all eat into it — but as a starting screen, 7.2% is the kind of yield the GTA hasn't offered on a small unit in a long time.

Why it's showing up here and not in Toronto: Ottawa's entry prices on 1-beds never ran as hot, and rents held. That spread is the whole game for a cashflow buyer. The catch worth naming out loud is condo fees — a high-fee building can turn a 7.2% gross into a mediocre net in a hurry, so this is a screen-then-underwrite number, not a buy signal.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty, a free Ontario housing-data tracker; the gross-yield-by-market figures are here → https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/rent-vs-buy. I built it because the boards don't surface yield at all — it's a number you're expected to reconstruct by hand, which conveniently keeps most buyers from ever running it.
