### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Whitby has the highest price-cut share of the 15 Ontario markets I track (32%) — and also the tightest supply. Someone explain this to me.

**Body:**

Pulled the numbers this morning across 15 GTA-area markets. Whitby stands out on two
metrics that shouldn't usually point the same way:

- 32% of active Whitby listings have already cut their asking price — highest of the
  15 (n=454). Typical reduction 6.7%.
- Whitby also has the *tightest* supply of the 15, at 3.1 months. Roughly 4–6 months is
  balanced; below that normally tilts toward sellers.

Worth being precise about that second one: months of supply is a ratio — active
inventory ÷ how fast homes are selling — not a raw house count. So "tightest" can come
from brisk sales just as much as from a thin pool of listings; it doesn't by itself mean
Whitby has the fewest homes for sale.

So it reads as a sellers' market on supply, but a third of sellers are marking down
anyway. One theory is that a chunk of inventory was listed at 2022-comp asking prices
and is now resetting to what the market will actually pay — the cut share measuring
optimism getting corrected rather than weakness in demand. But I'd genuinely like to be
told I'm reading it wrong.

One caveat on the other metric: "price-cut share" counts listings that dropped their
ask, not a same-home price change — a cut can just mean an ambitious first number.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them. Tracker's here
if you want to poke at it: https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/price-cuts

AMA on how it's calculated, or tell me where I'm wrong on the supply-vs-cuts read.

- **Region:** Richmond Hill · **Sample:** n=1,083
- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why surprising:** Longest median *true* days-on-market of the markets tracked today.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Median true days-on-market in Richmond Hill is now 80 days (n=1,083) — this is the un-reset clock, not the relist number

**Body:**

The typical active Richmond Hill listing has now sat 80 days — the longest median of the
markets I tracked today, across 1,083 active listings.

The word "true" is doing work here. On the board, days-on-market resets to zero every
time a listing is terminated and re-entered, so the public figure often reads far fresher
than the home actually is. This 80-day number stitches the listing history back together
so a relisted home keeps its original clock. That's the difference between "just came on"
and "has been trying since June."

Houses.ca and a few brokerages publish DOM too; the distinction I'm drawing is the
relist-stitching, not that the metric exists.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them. Tracker:
https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

Tell me where the stitching logic could be over- or under-counting.

*Self-score: 82/100 — the "true vs reset" method distinction is the credible core and it
names prior art. Slightly technical for the sub; the title carries it.*
