### A. Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Richmond Hill listing has now sat 104 days (median true DOM)

**Body:**
Ran the active Richmond Hill set: **the typical active Richmond Hill listing has now sat 104 days.** That's median *true* days-on-market across 1,173 listings — and it's the longest of any market I track right now.

The word that matters is *true*. The number a portal shows you resets every time a listing is cancelled and re-fired under a fresh MLS number, so a place that's actually been sitting since spring can read as "5 days new." Stitch the campaign history back together and Richmond Hill's real median is 104 days. Every one of those days is carrying cost the seller is eating — taxes, interest, the slow bleed. Time on market is the single cleanest proxy for how ready a seller is to deal.

The reset clock isn't a portal bug. It's the opaque MLS/board system deciding you don't get to see the campaign history.

### A. Reddit — r/ottawa

**Title:** A 1-bed in Ottawa grosses about 7.2% gross yield right now — best of the markets I track

**Body:**
Yield check for anyone running the buy-vs-rent math here: **a 1-bed in Ottawa grosses about 7.2% rental yield.** That's a 1-bed segment read across 4,083 listings, and it's the best gross yield of the Ontario markets I track today.

Gross yield is annual rent over purchase price before costs — so it's a ceiling, not your take-home, and financing and condo fees will eat into it. But as a starting screen it matters: when GTA cores are grinding out sub-5% gross, a 7.2% top-line is the difference between a deal that pencils and one that only works if you assume appreciation. Ottawa's been the quiet cashflow story while everyone stares at Toronto.

The frustrating part is that none of the standard portals put this number next to the listing. They'll show you the price and the photos and leave the math — the only thing an investor actually decides on — for you to do in a spreadsheet. That gap is the opaque board system, not a coincidence.
