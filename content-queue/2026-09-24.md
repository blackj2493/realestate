## Angle 2 — Hamilton days on market

- **Figure (verbatim):** 71 days
- **Region:** Hamilton
- **Sample:** n = 2,249 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why surprising:** longest median true days-on-market of the markets tracked today.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Hamilton listing has now sat 71 days (median true DOM) — longest of the markets I track today

**Body:**
> The median active Hamilton listing has been on the market 71 days by true days-on-market —
> the longest of the markets I'm tracking today (n = 2,249 active listings).
>
> The "true" part matters. Board DOM resets to zero when a listing is terminated and
> re-listed, so a home that's actually been sitting four months can show up as two weeks
> old. True DOM stitches those relists back together, which is why this number runs higher
> than what you'll see on a single listing page.
>
> Re-listing to reset the clock is a known tactic, and other tools have written about DOM
> games too — I'm not claiming to have invented the concept. The difference here is
> publishing the stitched median per market, refreshed nightly, so you can compare markets
> instead of guessing whether one listing's freshness is real.
>
> I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the
> board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.
>
> Tracker: https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
>
> AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — if you think relist-stitching over-counts, I'd like to
> hear the case.

**Self-score: 80/100.** The True-DOM method is the genuinely differentiated part and the
post leads with the figure then explains the method cleanly. Prior art named. Minor: could
