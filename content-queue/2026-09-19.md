- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature
- **Why it's surprising:** least inventory of the 15 markets tracked today — ≈4–6 months is balanced, and below that tilts hard toward sellers. It cuts against the blanket "GTA buyer's market" read.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Whitby is at 3.3 months of supply right now — the tightest of the 15 Ontario markets I track

**Body:**

Everyone's been calling 2026 a buyer's market, so this one surprised me: Whitby is sitting at **3.3 months of supply** — the lowest of the 15 Ontario markets in my dataset. Roughly 4–6 months is considered balanced; below that leans toward sellers. This is off 477 active listings, so it's a real sample, not a handful.

Months of supply is active inventory divided by the recent sales pace, so it's a demand-vs-inventory read, not a statement about where prices go next — a tight market can still see flat prices if buyers won't chase.

For context on prior art: TRREB reports months of inventory in its monthly Market Watch, but at the board level and monthly; the difference here is it's broken out per municipality and recomputed nightly, which is how a single suburb running against the regional narrative shows up at all.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — especially Durham folks: does 3.3 match what you're seeing on the ground in Whitby, or is inventory feeling looser than that?

- **Region:** Brampton
- **Sample:** 2,068 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/price-cuts
- **Why it's surprising:** highest price-cut share of the 15 markets tracked today.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** A third of active Brampton listings have already cut their asking price (typical cut 7.8%)

**Body:**

**33%** of active Brampton listings have reduced their asking price — the highest price-cut share of the 15 Ontario markets I track. When they cut, the typical reduction is **7.8%**. This is across 2,068 active listings.

Worth being precise about what this is: it's the share of *currently active* listings whose current ask is below their original ask. It's a snapshot of seller behaviour, not a sold-price stat — a cut doesn't mean the place sells at the reduced number, only that the first number missed.

Prior art, since people always ask: Realtor.ca flags individual "price reduced" listings and HouseSigma keeps price-change history per property. The difference here is aggregating it into a per-market share and pairing it with the typical cut size, refreshed nightly, so you can compare Brampton against 14 other markets in one view.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — Brampton buyers and agents, does a third of active listings cutting match what you're negotiating against?

- **Region:** Ottawa
- **Sample:** 4,304 listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/rent-vs-buy
- **Why it's surprising:** best gross rental yield of the markets tracked today (1-bed).

### (a) Reddit — r/ottawa

**Title:** Ottawa 1-beds are grossing about 6.9% rental yield — best of the Ontario markets I track

**Body:**

A 1-bed in Ottawa is grossing about **6.9% gross yield** right now — the best gross rental yield of the markets I track (1-bed segment), off 4,304 listings.

Be clear on what "gross" means: it's annual rent over price, before property tax, condo fees, vacancy, financing, or maintenance. Net yield is a very different, and lower, number. This is a top-line comparison across markets, not a pro-forma on any single unit.

Prior art: Rentals.ca and Urbanation publish Ontario rent levels monthly, and those are the better source for rent trends themselves. What's added here is pairing typical 1-bed rent against active list prices per market to get a comparable gross-yield figure, refreshed nightly.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — Ottawa landlords, does ~6.9% gross line up with what 1-beds actually rent and list for in your pocket of the city?
