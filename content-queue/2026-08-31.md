## Angle 2 — Oakville: typical active listing has sat 75 days

- **Figure (verbatim):** `75 days`
- **Region:** Oakville
- **Sample:** 1,060 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why surprising:** longest median true days-on-market of the markets tracked today.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Oakville listing has now sat 75 days — longest median true DOM of the markets I track

**Body:**

> Median true days-on-market for active Oakville listings is 75 days — the longest of any market in my dataset today, across 1,060 active listings.
>
> "True" is the load-bearing word. The days-on-market a listing shows you resets to zero every time an agent terminates and re-lists it, so the public number understates how long a property has actually been sitting. This stitches the history back together across re-lists. That's the difference between this and the DOM you see on a portal — same idea, different measurement.
>
> Oakville being the *slowest* is the surprise. It's an expensive market, and the assumption is that expensive means competitive. Here it means patient sellers who haven't met the market yet.
>
> Disclosure: I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.
>
> AMA — and if you're an Oakville agent and my re-list stitching is catching something it shouldn't, tell me where.

*Self-score: 84/100 — the "true DOM vs portal DOM" method distinction is genuinely differentiated, the counter-intuitive "expensive = slow" framing lands, honest about method limits. Strong.*
