- **Sample:** n = 1,064 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why surprising:** Longest median true days-on-market of the markets tracked today.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Richmond Hill listing has now sat 67 days — longest median of the markets I track

**Body:**

> Median **true** days-on-market for active Richmond Hill listings is **67 days**
> right now — the longest of the markets I track today. Sample is 1,064 active
> listings.
>
> The word "true" is doing the work here. The headline DOM a lot of portals show
> resets to zero when a listing is terminated and relisted, so a place that's
> actually been available for months can read as "3 days new." The 67-day figure
> is measured against first list date, not the latest relist, so it's trying to
> show how long inventory has genuinely been sitting.
>
> Caveat: it's a median of what's *currently active* — it's a snapshot of
> standing inventory, not an average of what sold. Slow movers are exactly the
> listings that pile up in an active snapshot, so read it as "how stale is the
> shelf," not "how long a sale takes."
>
> I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool —
> because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show
> them.
>
> Tracker: pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

## Angle 3 — Burlington months of supply

- **Figure (verbatim):** `3.2 months of supply`
- **Region:** Burlington
- **Sample:** n = 713 active listings
- **Source:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature
- **Why surprising:** Least inventory of the 15 markets tracked today — ≈4–6 months is balanced, and below that tilts hard toward sellers.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** While other GTA markets loosen, Burlington is at 3.2 months of supply — tightest of the 15 I track

**Body:**

> Months of supply measures how long it'd take to clear current inventory at the
> current sales pace. Roughly 4–6 months is considered balanced; below that
> tilts toward sellers. Burlington is sitting at **3.2 months of supply** right
> now — the tightest of the 15 markets I track. Sample is 713 active listings.
>
> What makes it stand out is the contrast: on the same dashboard other markets
> are showing price cuts and long days-on-market, i.e. loosening. Burlington is
> going the other way. "GTA is softening" is too coarse a statement to be true
> everywhere at once.
>
> Caveat: months-of-supply is a ratio of a stock (active listings) to a flow
> (recent sales pace), so it moves on either lever — it can tighten because
> demand firmed *or* because new listings dried up, and this figure alone doesn't
> separate the two.
>
> I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool —
> because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show
> them.
>
> Tracker: pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature
>
> AMA — Burlington folks, does 3.2 months square with how fast things are moving
> for you?

**Self-score: 82/100.** The cross-market contrast is the hook and the ratio
caveat is genuinely important. Loses a little for being the most "analyst" of the
