## Angle 2 — Oakville days-on-market

- **Figure (verbatim):** `76 days`
- **Region:** Oakville  ·  **Sample:** n=1086 active listings
- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market
- **Why it's surprising:** longest median true days-on-market of the markets tracked today.

### (a) Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** The typical active Oakville listing has now sat 76 days (n=1086) — longest median true DOM of the markets I track

**Body:**

The median active Oakville listing has now been on the market 76 days — the longest median true days-on-market of the markets I'm tracking, across n=1086 active listings.

The word doing the work is "true." A listing that's terminated and re-entered often shows a fresh DOM clock on the board feed even though the home has been available for months; HouseSigma and others surface DOM, but a relist can reset the number you see. I stitch the listing history so the clock reflects how long the home has actually been available, not how long since the last relist.

What makes 76 days notable is *where* it is. Oakville isn't a soft-demand market on paper — so a median this long says the gap between ask and what buyers will pay is wide enough that a lot of inventory is just sitting. For anyone hunting carrying-cost leverage, days-on-market is the first place motivated sellers show up.

This is a median across active listings, not sold homes, so it will run longer than a sold-DOM figure by construction — stale inventory is exactly what it's meant to capture.

I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (all markets, updates nightly): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — particularly on whether true DOM on active inventory is the right lens versus sold DOM.
