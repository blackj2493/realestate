# Which renovations show up in the sale price

## Figures (verbatim)

GTA house sales (detached, semi, town), Jul 2024 – Sep 2026, n = 56,299.
Each house compared only with houses in the same neighbourhood (CityRegion), same type,
same bedrooms, same size band, sold in the same quarter. Typical house = median price
$1,075,000.

| Feature | Price gap | At the median |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Extra bathroom | +5.3% | about $57K |
| Finished basement (vs unfinished) | +3.6% | about $38K |
| Basement apartment (vs unfinished) | +1.7% | about $18K |
| Listing says "fully renovated" | +5.1% | about $54K |
| Listing says fixer-upper / handyman | −12.3% | about −$132K |
| Separate entrance on its own | 0.0% | — |

- Bathroom gap in every city: +3.3% to +7.0%.
- Basement apartment added less than a plain finished basement in 11 of 12 cities
  (Brampton is the exception).
- Basement apartment: +3% to +5.5% in Oshawa, Toronto, Hamilton, Mississauga; no clear
  premium (95% range includes zero) in Markham, Richmond Hill, Oakville, Burlington, Whitby.
- "Fully renovated": +7.7% Hamilton, +6.4% Toronto, +1.6% Milton, +2.2% Richmond Hill.
- Basement 2-bed leases, Oct 2025 – Sep 2026: median $1,800/month, n = 1,940.
- Typical 2026 GTA costs (estimates from the tool's catalog, not quotes): bathroom
  $8–22K, finished basement $25–65K, full interior $30–110K, basement apartment $50–120K.

## Caveats

- Association, not cause: houses with more bathrooms are probably nicer in ways the data
  cannot see.
- "Fully renovated" and "fixer-upper" come from listing remarks.
- MLS does not say if a basement apartment is legal.
- City figures rest on 2,000–15,000 sales each.

## Draft (Reddit)

**Title:** Which renos show up in the sale price? 56,000 GTA house sales: extra bathroom ≈ +$57K, finished basement ≈ +$38K, basement apartment only ≈ +$18K [OC]

**Body:**

Which renovations actually show up when a house sells? We compared 56,299 GTA house sales from July 2024 to September 2026. We're PureProperty, an Ontario housing-data site run by a licensed Ontario realtor.

**How we compared:** each house was compared only with houses in the same neighbourhood, of the same type (detached, semi or town), with the same bedrooms and size range, sold in the same quarter.

**The numbers (typical GTA house, median price $1,075,000)**

- Extra bathroom: +5.3% (about $57K)
- Finished basement, vs unfinished: +3.6% (about $38K)
- Basement apartment, vs unfinished: +1.7% (about $18K)
- Listing says "fully renovated": +5.1% (about $54K)
- Listing says fixer-upper or handyman special: −12.3% (about −$132K)

**Against typical 2026 GTA costs**

- Bathroom, $8–22K: the price gap is several times the cost
- Finished basement, $25–65K: roughly break-even
- Full interior reno, $30–110K: break-even at best, on average
- Basement apartment, $50–120K: the sale price gap covers a fraction of the cost

**1. The bathroom stands out.** It had the biggest gap in every city, from +3.3% to +7.0%.

**2. A basement apartment shows up in rent, not in the sale price.** In 11 of 12 cities it added less at sale than a plain finished basement (Brampton was the exception). A separate entrance on its own added nothing. Basement 2-beds leased for a median of $1,800 a month last year (1,940 leases), so a $75K suite takes about 3.5 years of gross rent to cover its cost.

**3. The city changes the answer.** A basement apartment shows +3% to +5.5% in Oshawa, Toronto, Hamilton and Mississauga, and no clear premium in Markham, Richmond Hill, Oakville, Burlington or Whitby. "Fully renovated" is +7.7% in Hamilton and +6.4% in Toronto, but +1.6% in Milton.

**Caveats**

- This compares houses that have a feature with similar houses that don't. It doesn't prove that adding the feature causes the gap.
- "Fully renovated" and "fixer-upper" come from listing descriptions, and agents use the words loosely.
- MLS doesn't say if a basement apartment is legal.
- Costs are typical ranges, not quotes. City numbers rest on 2,000–15,000 sales each.
