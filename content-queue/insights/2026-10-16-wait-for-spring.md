# Is it worth waiting for spring to sell?

Timely in October: owners ask whether to list now or wait for spring.

## Figures (verbatim)

GTA freehold sales (detached, semi, freehold town; Toronto + 17 GTA cities),
deals signed Jul 2024 – Sep 2026, n = 65,143.

Method: same-home comparison. Each sale is compared within its cell (city ×
type × bedrooms × size band, 870 cells with 5+ sales), with a dummy for the month the
deal was signed and a linear time trend.

- Same-home price by month signed (average month = 0): Jan −1.6%, Feb +0.2%, Mar +1.3%,
  Apr +2.8%, May +2.9%, Jun +1.7%, Jul −0.7%, Aug −1.2%, Sep −0.3%, Oct −1.4%,
  Nov −1.4%, Dec −2.2%.
- Spring premium: a deal signed in April–May is about 4% higher than the same home
  signed in October–November.
- Trend over the period: −5.9% a year. At that pace, the seven months from October to
  May cost about 3.4%.
- Net, if the trend holds: about +1% for waiting, before carrying costs.
- Competition: homes listed in April that sold = 7,593; in October = 4,545
  (two-year totals, listed Jul 2024 – Jun 2026). About 1.7× as many.
- Sold over asking, by listing month: April 28.7%, October 21.9%.
- Sale price against the first asking price is flat all year: 97.0–97.7% for every
  listing month.

## Caveats

- Only two springs in the data (2025, 2026). The seasonal pattern is an average of two.
- The −5.9% trend is the past two years. Nobody knows the next seven months; if prices
  flatten, spring wins by about 4%; if they fall faster, waiting loses.
- Sold data only: homes that never sold (expired, terminated) are not in it.
- Carrying costs (mortgage, tax, a second home) are not in the figure.
- This is what the data shows, not advice for any one seller.

## Prior art

TRREB and CREA publish seasonally adjusted price indexes (MLS® HPI), so the seasonal
pattern itself is known. The difference here: the spring premium is set against the
trend in one number, for the same kind of home.
