## Angle 2 — Hamilton: the typical active listing has now sat 70 days

- **Figure (verbatim):** `70 days`
- **Sample:** n = 2,163 active Hamilton listings
- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

### Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** Median *true* days-on-market for active Hamilton listings is now 70 days — and that clock doesn't reset on relist

**Body:**

Median true days-on-market for currently-active Hamilton listings just hit **70 days** (n=2,163). That's the longest of the markets I track right now.

The word doing the work is *true*. Boards publish an average DOM figure, but the MLS clock resets to zero every time a listing is cancelled and relisted — a stale property gets a fresh "1 day on market" and looks like new inventory. This number stitches the relists back together, so 70 days means the property has actually been sitting for about 70 days regardless of how many times it's been re-papered.

Why a principal should care: DOM is your negotiating leverage gauge. A seller at day 70 has carried two-plus months of taxes, interest, and insurance with no offer. That's a very different conversation than a seller at day 7. When the median crosses two months, you're negotiating with a market that's tired, not hot.

Disclosure: I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (median true DOM, all markets, daily): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/days-on-market

AMA / tell me where I'm wrong — especially if you think stitching relists overstates DOM for legitimately re-strategized listings.

## Angle 3 — Whitby: 3.0 months of supply

- **Figure (verbatim):** `3.0 months of supply`
- **Sample:** n = 440 active Whitby listings
- **Source URL:** https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature

### Reddit — r/TorontoRealEstate

**Title:** While Brampton and Hamilton soften, Whitby is sitting at 3.0 months of supply — tightest of the markets I track

**Body:**

Interesting split in the data today. Same week Brampton has a third of listings cutting price and Hamilton's median true DOM is 70 days, **Whitby is at 3.0 months of supply** (n=440) — the least inventory of the 15 markets I track.

Rule of thumb most desks use: roughly 4–6 months of supply is balanced. Below that tilts toward sellers; well above it toward buyers. Whitby at 3.0 is on the sellers' side while a lot of the GTA is visibly loosening.

Why it matters for a principal: "the GTA market" is not one thing. If you're underwriting off a headline regional average, you'll over-pay in Whitby (still competitive) and under-bid in Brampton (softening) — same day, same region. Months-of-supply per municipality, refreshed daily, is how you keep the two straight. CREA and TRREB publish months-of-inventory monthly and region-wide; the value here is the daily, per-market cut.

Disclosure: I'm a licensed Ontario realtor and I built PureProperty — a free data tool — because the board sits on numbers like this and the consumer sites won't show them.

Tracker (months of supply by market, daily): https://www.pureproperty.ca/data/market-temperature
