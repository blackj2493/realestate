## 0. Reading this document

This is your operating plan, not a manifesto. It is calibrated to the only three facts that actually constrain you: **(1)** you are one person who builds on nights and weekends; **(2)** you hold an Ontario salesperson licence under EXP/Katherine Milian, which is both your cash register *and* the thread the whole data licence hangs from; **(3)** your build velocity is abundant (50 commits/month via agents) but your *human transaction-servicing hours* and your *distribution* are scarce and cannot be bought.

## 1. The one-line strategy + definition of winning

**One-line strategy:** Become the mandatory pre-offer underwriting terminal for flippers and value-add investors in a 1–2 city slice of the 905 belt, then monetize that captive high-intent audience through *your own buyer-side commissions on the few deals you can personally service, brokerage-to-brokerage referral fees on the many you can't, and a thin Pro subscription floor* — never by out-trafficking HouseSigma.

**Definition of winning (the 24-month metric — pick the realistic line, not the heroic one):**

> By month 24, PureProperty generates **≥ $250K in founder take-home** in the trailing 12 months, composed of **6–8 personally-serviced buyer-side closes** (~$140–185K net under EXP's *real* 80/20-to-cap economics, corrected below), **20–30 brokerage-to-brokerage referred closes** (~$130–195K gross referral fees), and **150–300 Pro subscribers** (~$70–210K ARR) — off a base of **300–600 engaged gated accounts in the target micro-market**, with the underlying TRREB feed never having gone dark for >24h.

Note what this is NOT: it is not "2M users," not "out-rank HouseSigma on Google," not "10–26 closes I personally run." Winning is **revenue-per-high-intent-user**, and the millionaire trajectory (Section 4) comes from *origination leverage*, not from your personal calendar — because your calendar is a hard cap of ~8 closes/year and pretending otherwise is the single most expensive lie on the board.

## 2. Where to play — the beachhead

### The exact niche
- **Segment:** GTA **flippers and small BRRRR / value-add investors** (1–4 unit freehold buyers, the "force-appreciation" buyer). NOT condos, NOT the mass homebuyer, NOT cashflow-only (rent model is only ~47% covered — that's the *next* expansion, not the beachhead), NOT builders/developers (hollow; no PostGIS; explicitly cut).
- **Geography:** Start in **ONE primary city — Brampton — plus one adjacent overflow market (Mississauga OR Durham/Oshawa).** The red-team is right that "3–4 cities" is already too wide for a solo operator whose only follower-proof edge is relationship depth. Go narrow enough that you can be *the* name every Brampton flipper says on deal night. Widen only on the trigger in Section 6.
- **Use-case:** *Pre-offer distress underwriting* — "is this seller bleeding, what's the real time-on-market, and what does the rehab actually return in THIS micro-market, before I write."

### Why it's narrow enough to dominate yet valuable
One personally-serviced GTA buyer-side close is worth ~$26.5K gross. You do not need a big audience — you need a few hundred *engaged* accounts in two cities. Crucially, **investor/flipper buyers are the cheapest segment in real estate to represent**: they underwrite remotely, want one or two confirmatory walk-throughs (not 30 emotional Saturday showings), and move on data. This is the rare segment whose servicing profile actually fits a nights-and-weekends licence.

### **MANDATORY Phase-0 gate: size the niche before building anything (red-team hole #1 + #13).**
The entire plan assumes ~300–600 accounts → ~26–38 transactions/year. **That assumption is unsized and could be fatal.** Before Phase 1, do a bottom-up TAM count from aggregates you already have (no raw records — counts and medians only, fully compliant):
- Count trailing-12-month **1–4 unit freehold sales in Brampton + the overflow city** (you have this in VOW aggregate).
- Apply an investor-share estimate of **10–20%**.
- Cross-reference against **active GTA REI-meetup / flipper-group membership** (public).
- **Kill/narrow rule:** if the credibly-reachable, solo-agent-willing investor population in the two cities is **< 200 people**, do NOT proceed on the income target as written — either widen geography by one city *first*, or downgrade the 24-month metric to the referral-only base case (Section 4). Run the conversion math at **1% / 2% / 4%** (low/mid/high), and gate every downstream dollar figure on the *measured* Phase-2 number, never the borrowed one.

### The structural reason HouseSigma can't follow — stated honestly (red-team hole #7)
The committee's "HouseSigma is *structurally forbidden* from shipping this" claim is **too strong and is now corrected.** HouseSigma already ships relisting/termination flags and price-cut history; it monetizes *buyer* leads too; the cheapest incumbent response is a "cumulative DOM" badge that is 80% as good to a 2M-MAU audience and neutralizes your *marketing* punch in a quarter.

So the honest competitive claim is narrower and more durable:
- **What's true:** HouseSigma's incentive (volume-convert the mass market to its own listing-and-buy funnel) means it will keep its product *broad and reassuring*; it has little reason to invest in *rigorous* relist-chain collapse for a tiny investor sub-segment, and doing so mildly antagonizes the listing agents it courts. You can be **sharper and faster on this one axis** than they'll bother to be.
- **What you must NOT bet the company on:** that they "can't" copy it. **Treat True DOM as a customer-acquisition hook with a ~2-quarter shelf life before parity, not as the moat.** The moat is Section 3. Pre-commit: *if any competitor ships a true/cumulative-DOM badge within 12 months, the response is to deepen proprietary-data lock-in (the closed-deal dataset), not to re-sharpen the copyable signal.*

## 3. How to win — the moat

You have one *acquisition hook* (which is copyable) and two *durable moats* (which are not). Be ruthlessly clear about which is which.

### Hook (copyable, ~2-quarter shelf life): Relist-corrected True DOM
Already shipped (212k stitched chains, deterministic ETL, migration 018). It is the best *top-of-funnel* you have because it's vivid, free to run, and HouseSigma won't bother matching its rigor for a while. **Lead every public surface with it — but never call it the moat.**

### Durable Moat #1: Micro-market relationship depth
A solo operator's *only* follower-proof asset. A funded licensee (Wahi, a well-resourced team — red-team hole #8) has identical VOW access, more capital, and full-time staff. Capital erodes a broad brand fastest; it erodes *being the trusted name in Brampton flipper circles* slowest. This is why the geography is 1–2 cities, not 3–4. **You win by being un-buyably deep, not broad.**

### Durable Moat #2: The closed-deal / renovation-outcome dataset — *decoupled from your own closings* (resolves red-team hole #9, the timing inversion)
The genuinely non-copyable asset is a calibration set of *actual* rehab-cost → resale → margin outcomes in your micro-market, which sharpens the renovation-ROI engine (your real differentiator over HouseSigma's homebuyer-grade AVM) into something no portal can match.

**The trap the red-team exposed:** at ~6–8 founder-serviced closes/year, a 50-deal proprietary set takes 3–5 years — by which time a follower has copied the front-end and out-distributed you. **The fix is to stop sourcing the dataset from your own closes.** Build a lightweight, *user-contributed* outcome-capture flow: engaged users log their deal outcomes (purchase, reno spend, resale/refi) **even on deals you referred away or that a competitor brokered.** Now the dataset compounds with *engaged-account count*, not with your calendar — decoupling moat growth from the hard cap. This is the single most important structural move in the plan for long-term defensibility.

### The licence-vs-neutrality tension — RESOLVED
The prior council prized "no brokerage = no conflict." **That is dead: you ARE an agent.** Pretending neutrality is legally fragile (RECO Bulletin 3.5) and a lie flippers will smell.

**Resolution: weaponize disclosure; sell competence, not Switzerland.** Positioning: *"Built by a licensed Ontario investor-agent who underwrites his own deals on this terminal — and shows you the numbers the portals smooth over."* The "neutral" claim is about the **data being unspun** (we don't hide distress to protect listings), never about having no commercial interest. The RECO conflict disclosure lives in the Terms-of-Use gate you are *already building* — captured once, at the same consent step that satisfies the VOW bona-fide-consumer test. Honesty here doubles as marketing: an authentic insider beats a faceless incumbent in every community channel, which is the only distribution you can afford.

**⚠️ Load-bearing precondition (red-team hole #10 — DO NOT skip):** the referral-fee structure and the "agent-operated neutral terminal" brand are *asserted* legal but **never validated by RECO/TRESA-competent counsel.** Under TRESA/REBBA, *all* remuneration connected to a trade — referral fees, lead-gen fees, and arguably Pro-subscription revenue tied to representation — must flow through EXP, not to you personally or to an unregistered "PureProperty" entity. Routing any such dollar to yourself/a non-registrant company is unlicensed-trading exposure that **RECO (not TRREB) enforces, and it can revoke the registration the entire VOW edge depends on.** This is a hard Phase-0 gate, ranked *above* the feed fixes (Section 5).

## 4. The monetization model + the millionaire math

### The corrected commission engine (red-team / brokerage-insider correction — material)
The committee modeled "50/50 split ≈ $13K net." **EXP Canada is 80/20 with a US$16K cap, then 100%.** On a $1.06M GTA buyer-side close at 2.5% = ~$26.5K gross:
- **Deal 1–3 (pre-cap):** you keep ~$21.2K each (80%), the 20% paying down the ~US$16K cap.
- **Deal 4+ (post-cap, same calendar year):** you keep ~$26K each, minus ~$250/deal.

This **raises** every founder-income number by 50–95% versus the committee's tables and *further widens* the gap between one close and a year of subscriptions.

### The three revenue rails, sequenced by $/hour-of-your-scarce-time

| Rail | Unit economics | Your time cost | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Direct buyer-side commission** (you rep) | ~$21–26K net/close | HIGH (daytime, full-attention) | The *quality* tier. Hard-capped by your calendar at **~6–8 closes/yr**. |
| **2. Brokerage-to-brokerage referral** (you source, others rep) | ~$6.6K/close (25% of co-op), brokerage-to-brokerage via EXP | ~ZERO ongoing | The *scale* tier and the **base case for the millionaire path**. Monetizes the leads your calendar physically rejects. |
| **3. Pro subscription** | $59/mo (anchored to PropStream/PropTrackr $49) | LOW (after billing built) | The *recurring floor* that funds infra between lumpy closes AND pre-qualifies leads. NOT the lead engine. |

### The millionaire math — referral-rail as BASE case, not upside (resolves red-team holes #5, #13)

The committee loaded the millionaire claim onto a **Year-2 team rail** (recruit/train 1–2 EXP agents to service sourced overflow). The red-team is right: agent management is *event-driven weekday* people-ops that collides with the day-job firewall, and a single mis-served sourced lead damages Moat #1. **So the team rail is NOT the base case.** The base case is *pure brokerage-to-brokerage referral*, the only genuinely day-job-compatible scaling line (zero founder weekday hours, zero people-ops, zero reputation-by-proxy risk).

**Run the table at measured conversion (gate everything on the Phase-2 number):**

Assume Year-2 engaged accounts in the two cities = **500**, and a *blended* account-to-originated-transaction rate. Show three scenarios because the rate is unvalidated:

| Scenario | Conv. rate | Originated deals/yr | You service (cap ~7) | Referred (rest) | Founder net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Low** | 1% | 5 | 5 (~$110K) | 0 | **~$110K** + subs |
| **Base** | 3% | 15 | 7 (~$155K) | 8 (~$53K) | **~$208K** + subs |
| **High** | 5% | 25 | 7 (~$155K) | 18 (~$119K) | **~$274K** + subs |

Add the subscription floor (150–300 subs @ $59 = **$106–212K ARR**, *net of EXP-flow once counsel confirms structure*):

- **Base case total: ~$208K commission/referral + ~$106K subs ≈ $315K founder take-home.** High income, day-job-compatible, no team, no own-brokerage.
- **High case: ~$274K + ~$210K ≈ $480K.**
- **The millionaire trajectory** = the High case *compounding* over years 2–4 as the engaged-account base grows from 500 → 1,500+ (referral deals scale linearly with accounts since they cost you ~zero time; subs scale with accounts), **plus** the optional team rail layered in *only after* referral volume alone proves a backlog so large it justifies the management hour — and even then, hire **ONE experienced agent who needs no QC**, never train juniors.

**The honest base case if conversion comes in low or your calendar tightens: this is a $150–200K/yr lifestyle origination business with a copyable front-end — high income, not venture-defensible.** That outcome is acceptable and must be stated up front so you don't over-invest against a millionaire number that requires the High scenario to hold.

### IF/WHEN to stand up your own brokerage
**Not for 24+ months, possibly never.** It requires the broker exam, 24 months' registered experience, a separate RECO registration, **and re-papering BOTH IDX and VOW agreements from zero under a new BoR (the VOW licence does NOT transfer).** That single move can kill the company. **Trigger to even revisit:** sustained **≥15–20 personally-serviced-equivalent closes/year** where EXP's 20%-to-cap is demonstrably the binding cost. Until then, stay under EXP; the intermediate scaling move is a *team under EXP* (no new RECO paper, Katherine stays the liability backstop), gated as above.

## 5. The step-by-step roadmap from TODAY

3. **Resilience-by-deferral, not just alerting (red-team hole #4):** the silent-cursor sync bug is existential AND a feed outage is unstaffed weekday work. Ship: **(a)** serve last-good cached data with a visible "as of" timestamp so a stale feed degrades gracefully (and never breaches 24h freshness silently); **(b)** auto-pause public CTAs/acquisition when the staleness alert fires so no lead lands on a broken surface; **(c)** a one-page mechanical runbook so recovery is a single weekend-deferrable action; **(d)** the dead-man's-switch alert itself.
4. **Compliance pre-flight (corrected to current ground-truth — red-team hole #12):** the headline §6.3(c) compare "breach" is **already closed** (verified: `compareMetricsConfig.ts` renders Brokerage as an `alwaysShow` row; `/share/[token]` reads only the active `listings` table, shows `ListOfficeName`, carries the bona-fide notice). - **Gate to Phase 1:** clean legal opinion in hand + TAM ≥ 200 + feed degrades gracefully + terms enforcement verified live.

### PHASE 1 — The minimum-viable monetization event (1 sprint / ~2 weeks)
**Goal:** make the product capable of producing one buyer-rep conversation. This is "one button and a disclosure away from revenue."
1. Wire **one disclosed CTA — "Have the analyst who built this underwrite your offer"** — onto the gated distress/True-DOM/reno-ROI surfaces, firing on the highest-intent action (positive reno-ROI result, sold-comps-gate hit, saved distress comp).

### PHASE 2 — Validate conversion by hand (3–4 sprints / ~6–8 weeks). *The single most important phase.*
**Goal:** prove that data-loving flippers will actually let a solo agent rep them — the one assumption the whole income model rests on and that nobody has measured. **Build NOTHING new this phase.**

### PHASE 3 — Distribution + recurring floor (ongoing; the hardest problem, sequenced last because it's most fragile)

### Residual holes unresolved after 3 hardening rounds


**CRITICAL**

- **The 300-600 engaged-account assumption is an aspiration, not a sized market** - The income model multiplies 500 accounts by a conversion rate. That 500 traces to the persona advocate's R0 line earn the obsession of 500 of them, a motivational target never counted as a population. Reachable TAM is not all Brampton flippers; it is those active this year who discover a solo terminal, lack a trusted flipper-agent, and will hand a six-figure buy-side mandate to a weekend licensee. The filters are multiplicative; survivors could be a few dozen, making the beachhead a rounding error against the fixed cost the plan front-loads. _Fix:_ Make the Phase-0 TAM gate a bottom-up funnel: trailing-12mo 1-4-unit freehold sales times investor-share times repeat-buyer rate times un-agented rate. If the reachable-and-switchable count is under 150, drop the millionaire framing from the document.
- **The referral rail — the entire 'day-job-compatible scaling' base case — is itself unstaffed weekday human work, not zero-touch** - The plan's whole millionaire math rests on referral being '~ZERO ongoing time' so it scales without consuming the founder's calendar. That is false in practice: a brokerage-to-brokerage referral still requires same-day lead qualification, an intro/handoff call, picking and vetting the receiving agent, and follow-up to actually collect the fee on close — all of which happen on weekday business hours when a lead is hot, exactly when the founder is at his day job. A flipper lead that waits until Saturday evening for a callback is a dead lead. So the 'scale tier' that's supposed to absorb the 8-25 deals/yr the calendar rejects is gated by the SAME weekday-hour scarcity as direct rep, just at lower intensity per deal. The plan's claim that referral is the safe, hours-free base case is the load-bearing assumption and it is not validated. _Fix:_ Treat referral as low-touch-but-not-zero-touch and time-budget it explicitly (e.g. ~1-2 weekday hours per live lead). Either (a) pre-negotiate a standing referral agreement with ONE trusted EXP overflow agent who takes the handoff call so the founder only needs an async intro, or (b) build an automated intake-to-agent routing step (lead fills form -> auto-assigned -> founder confirms async that evening) so the only synchronous weekday act is a one-line approval. Without one of these, cut the High/Base referral volumes in the millionaire table by at least half.
- **The user-contributed deal dataset (Durable Moat #2) loses to whoever has the most users — and that is structurally never the solo founder** - The plan's whole long-term defensibility rests on outcome-capture compounding with engaged-account count rather than the founder's calendar. But a contributed dataset is a network-effect asset, and network-effect assets accrue to the largest distribution, not the first mover. HouseSigma already has 2M MAU and the exact same relationship to these users; the instant the flipper-outcome-logging idea is shown to work, they can bolt a 'log your deal' flow onto their app and out-collect the founder's two-city set in a single quarter — 2M users contributing > 500 users contributing, permanently. Worse, the reno-ROI engine the dataset is meant to sharpen is itself derived from VOW hedonic coefficients (src/lib/avm/valueAdd/engine.ts) that any licensed competitor reproduces from the same feed; the contributed data is a thin calibration layer on a copyable base, not a proprietary base. A solo operator winning a density race against a 4000x-larger audience is the core competitive fallacy here. _Fix:_ Stop framing the contributed dataset as a defensible moat against incumbents — it is a retention feature, not a wall. Re-anchor the ONLY genuine moat on the one thing distribution cannot buy: the founder's personal trust+transaction relationship with named individual flippers (Moat #1), and accept the company is defensible only at personal-income scale. Concretely: gate the deal-outcome data so it is NEVER exportable/portable and only valuable *inside* a serviced/referred relationship, so a competitor copying the flow gets cold-start data with no relationship to monetize it.
- **The VOW licence almost certainly does NOT run to a 'PureProperty' brand at all — it runs to the brokerage's PROPTX VOW for servicing ITS consumers, and an agent-operated public analytics site is a different animal the BoR must personally underwrite** - The plan repeatedly calls the licence 'the thread the whole company hangs from' and 'held by someone else,' but never confronts what that means: TRREB VOW/IDX agreements are signed by the Member brokerage (EXP) and its Broker-of-Record, who under §11.1 is 'fully and directly liable for any breach' — not by a salesperson and certainly not by an unregistered 'PureProperty.ca' entity. A VOW is licensed to display the brokerage's listings to bona-fide consumers OF THAT BROKERAGE for the purpose of providing brokerage services. A standalone, publicly-marketed, subscription-gated analytics terminal operated by one salesperson, funneling leads the brokerage never originated, is a materially different use that a prudent BoR may simply refuse to put their registration behind once they understand the liability — or PROPTX may decline at the URL-registration step (max 3 URLs/feed). The plan's 'Phase-0 legal gate' frames this as a question of HOW remuneration flows; the prior, unasked question is WHETHER the BoR will license a public analytics brand under their VOW at all. If the answer is no, there is no product. This dependency is held entirely by EXP/Katherine/Tanmay. _Fix:_ Before any feature or income math, get a WRITTEN, signed authorization from the EXP Broker-of-Record (and PROPTX URL registration) specifically scoping 'a public, subscription-and-lead-gen analytics site under the brokerage VOW, operated by [founder]' — not a generic VOW. Treat a refusal-to-sign as the true kill gate, ranked above the RECO opinion. If the BoR will only authorize gated-use-for-our-own-consumers (the original CLAUDE.md/memory constraint), the public-terminal + SEO + share-loop surfaces collapse to logged-in-consumer-only, which guts the entire distribution thesis — better to learn that in week one than after building.
- **Routing referral/subscription revenue 'through EXP' does not cure the unlicensed-trading and holding-out exposure — and the plan treats counsel as a checkbox rather than a structural redesign that may invalidate the model** - Under TRESA, a salesperson cannot be remunerated for a trade-related service except by their brokerage, and an unregistered entity (a 'PureProperty' Inc.) cannot receive trade-connected remuneration at all (the personal-real-estate-corporation rules are narrow and don't cover a software/lead-gen company). The plan's mitigation — 'route all trade-connected remuneration through EXP' — assumes EXP will agree to pass referral fees and a slice of SaaS revenue back out to the founder/his company, AND that doing so survives the holding-out and disclosure rules: a licensed salesperson publicly operating a 'neutral terminal' that solicits representation while not foregrounding the brokerage's name risks a RECO holding-out/false-advertising finding (the brand is 'PureProperty,' not 'EXP'). The plan's 'weaponize disclosure' answer is good marketing but is not a legal cure for who-can-be-paid-what. RECO (not TRREB) enforces this, and a finding can suspend/revoke the registration the VOW hangs on — a second, independent kill-switch. Calling this a 'Phase-0 gate' understates it: the answer may force the SaaS revenue to belong to EXP, or force the brand to be EXP-affiliated on its face, either of which rewrites Section 4's economics. _Fix:_ Commission the counsel opinion as a structural design exercise, not a yes/no: have it specify (a) which revenue streams MUST flow through EXP vs. which (pure software subscription with zero representation tie) can legitimately accrue to a separate non-registrant SaaS entity; (b) the exact brand/affiliation disclosure required so 'PureProperty' isn't a holding-out breach (likely 'PureProperty, a service of [founder], salesperson, EXP Realty'); (c) whether a brokerage-to-brokerage referral fee on a lead the founder never represented is even a 'trade' he can be paid for. Re-run the income table AFTER that opinion, because the answer may strip the subscription rail of its margin or require it to sit in EXP. Until then, the $315K base case is built on a remuneration structure that may be illegal as drawn.
- **Subscription ARR ($106–212K) is fantasy revenue: implied free-to-paid conversion is 5–10× normal, and it's priced above a broader competitor** - 150–300 retained paying subs off 300–600 engaged accounts implies 25–50% free-to-paid conversion vs. a 2–5% SaaS norm — a 5–10× overshoot. Worse, the plan anchors to PropStream at $49 yet prices at $59 for a TWO-CITY tool, while PropStream offers nationwide data + skip-tracing; you are pricing above a vastly broader product. Churn is also structurally severe: a single-purpose investor tool is only useful during an active deal hunt, so subscribers pause/cancel between deals (high involuntary churn unmodeled). Realistic retained-sub revenue is ~$20–40K, not $106–212K — which removes roughly a third of the 'base case' total and most of the gap to the millionaire line. Zero billing infra exists today (grep confirms 'Stripe/subscription' appears only in docs), so this is entirely unbuilt and unvalidated. _Fix:_ Re-baseline subs at a 3% free-to-paid conversion and a 30–40% annual churn assumption; model it as a thin floor (~$20–40K) NOT a revenue pillar, exactly as Rail 3's stated role — then delete it from the headline 'base case total' so the $315K figure isn't propped up by it. Price BELOW $49 (e.g. $29) given the two-city scope, or gate the Pro tier behind demonstrated retention in Phase 2's probe before assuming any ARR.

**MAJOR**

- **Named acquisition channels are for the wrong persona** - Growth research names BiggerPockets Canada, REIN, and Ontario landlord Facebook groups but calls them cashflow-investor watering holes. The strategy demotes Cashflow to destination and makes Flipper the beachhead, so the only concrete channels feed a benched persona, while flippers congregate in tighter, relationship-gated, often-private circles with no identified scalable channel. Seeding 5-10 anchors is demand discovery, not generation, and does not scale past the founder's evenings. _Fix:_ Before Phase 1, name specific Brampton/905 flipper Discords/FB groups, confirm member counts and vendor tolerance. If the only public investor channels are cashflow-flavored, let the channel pick the beachhead: lead with Cashflow and use Flipper distress as the hook.
- **Investor buyers being cheap to service cuts against demand** - Flippers underwriting remotely with one or two walk-throughs is what makes 6-8 personal closes and the referral rail feasible. But a buyer needing no hand-holding feels almost no switching cost and little lock-in, so rep-intent (KPI 4) is most likely to come in low. Sophisticated flippers are the segment least likely to abandon their existing agent for a side-project terminal, because the data edge is copyable in two quarters and gives no representation advantage. High intent is not high willingness-to-switch-rep, and the model needs the latter. _Fix:_ In Phase 2 measure rep-switch intent: how many engaged flippers move their NEXT deal off their current agent? Treat below 5 of cohort as a kill signal. Pre-commit that if switch-intent is weak but tool usage strong, the model is subscription/data-primary, not origination-primary.
- **The concurrent operational surface for one part-time operator is already at or past capacity BEFORE any monetization is layered on** - Verified in code: the nightly job is a 7-step chain (core sync + 6 continue-on-error refresh scripts: condo-fee stats, sale history, sqft calibration, trend+offset, property estimates, watchlist alerts), several of which have NEVER successfully run in CI (the workflow itself notes prior jobs always died at the sync step). On top sit an offline Colab AVM fitter (single point of failure, plan grades it MINOR), Typesense schema/reindex, the magic-link auth + alerts, and the VOW compliance gate. Each is a thing that can silently break on a Tuesday and that only the founder understands. The plan's Phase-0 'resilience-by-deferral' (cached last-good + auto-pause CTAs + runbook) is correctly scoped but does NOT yet exist in the codebase (no staleness-banner/last-good cache layer found), so today a feed or refresh-step failure during work hours degrades the product with no graceful fallback — directly undercutting the 'lead never lands on a broken surface' guarantee the monetization depends on. _Fix:_ Before Phase 1, do an honest ops-hours census: list every recurring script/system and its mean-time-to-recover for a solo operator, then RUTHLESSLY consolidate or freeze. Specifically: verify the 6 continue-on-error steps have actually run green at least once (the workflow admits several never have), or disable the unproven ones so they can't mask a real failure. Build the graceful-degradation cache + 'as of' timestamp banner as the literal first code commit of Phase 0, since the entire CTA model assumes it exists. Version the Colab notebook in-repo now (it's a true SPOF, not minor).
- **Phase 2 'validate by hand' collides head-on with the day-job firewall and has no time budget that survives a single deal or outage** - Phase 2 — the plan's self-described single most important phase — requires the founder to manually service 1-3 real end-to-end buyer deals AND run in-person meetup/Discord seeding AND stand up deal-outcome capture, all while NOT building. Servicing even one live buyer transaction is dozens of weekday hours (showings, offer night, conditions, financing, closing) that cannot be deferred to a weekend. The plan acknowledges recruiting hours must be 'carved out of build hours,' but there is no slack left to carve: a single active deal plus one Tuesday-afternoon feed outage will consume an entire month's evening budget, stalling both recruiting AND the conversion measurement the gate depends on. The risk is that Phase 2 never completes cleanly enough to read the kill/scale signal, leaving the founder stuck servicing one deal indefinitely with no validated funnel. _Fix:_ Sequence Phase 2 around the day job, not in parallel with it: validate PRICE-intent and REP-intent (the $59 card + the verbal pre-commit) FIRST and as the gate, since those are async/evening-compatible and answer the load-bearing question, and defer actually-servicing live deals until at least one paid-or-committed cohort exists. Explicitly book the first real close during a planned PTO/vacation block rather than assuming evenings absorb it. If the founder cannot take ~2 weeks of flexible daytime within the next 6 months, the direct-rep rail is not executable and the model must be declared referral-and-subscription-only from the start.
- **'Relationship depth' is contested by other licensed agents, not by HouseSigma — and the plan never models that fight** - The competitive lens the plan defends against is the *portal* (HouseSigma/Wahi). But the actual threat to 'be THE name every Brampton flipper says on deal night' is the 50+ other licensed agents and agent-influencers already farming that exact community with Instagram reels, REI-meetup sponsorships, and free underwriting — several of whom can also get VOW access and clone the True-DOM hook with a contractor in a weekend. The plan treats relationship depth as 'un-buyably deep' and capital-resistant, but it is precisely what a well-funded local team buys: they outspend on the meetups, sponsor the Discord, and hire a dev. The founder's structural disadvantage (nights/weekends, ~8 closes/yr capacity) means a full-time competitor can simply be MORE present in the same rooms. The moat erodes not from above (portal) but laterally (peer licensees), which is unmodeled. _Fix:_ Add a peer-licensee competitive trigger to Section 6/7 watchlist (not just 'funded follower'): monitor for any local agent/team shipping a distress/True-DOM tool in the target city. The defensible response is the contributed-data + servicing flywheel that a part-time competitor can't staff AND a full-time generalist agent won't bother to build — i.e. lean into the *engineering-x-licence* combination that is rare among local agents, and pick the ONE city where no tech-forward agent-influencer already dominates the flipper channel (verify this in the Phase-0 TAM gate, not after launch).
- **True DOM's copy-time is over-estimated at 2 quarters; an incumbent can ship a 'cumulative DOM' badge in weeks, collapsing the only working acquisition channel** - The plan budgets ~2 quarters of shelf life and treats that as comfortable runway to build the data moat. But the stitching logic is a 35-day-window relist-chain merge over data the incumbent already holds (the engine is ~500 lines, deterministic, no ML) — a motivated competitor ships an 80%-as-good badge in weeks once it sees the marketing traction, not quarters. Since the plan ALSO concedes distribution is the unsolved problem and True DOM is the primary top-of-funnel hook, parity on the hook doesn't just blunt marketing — it removes the founder's single working acquisition argument before the slow data moat has had time to compound (the timing inversion the plan claims to have fixed actually re-opens here: hook dies fast, moat matures slow, and the contributed-data fix needs the hook alive to drive the account growth that feeds it). _Fix:_ Treat the hook as already-parity and do not let acquisition depend on it surviving. Diversify the top-of-funnel NOW onto something genuinely slower to copy: the founder's *personal underwriting voice* (an insider doing public teardowns of specific deals with the licence + numbers, which a portal legally won't and a generalist agent can't), and warm-community seeding that converts before any badge war matters. Pre-commit (as the plan half-does) that if a competitor ships cumulative-DOM within 6 months, acquisition pivots to person-led content, not a feature arms race the solo founder cannot win.
- **The user-contributed deal-outcome dataset (Moat #2) and the 'neutral insider' positioning create a fresh conflict-of-interest / fiduciary-disclosure surface the plan never compliance-checks** - The plan's single most important structural move — having engaged users log purchase/reno/resale outcomes, including on deals the founder referred away — collides with two duties it doesn't address. First, the founder is simultaneously (a) a representative who owes fiduciary duties to clients he reps, (b) the operator of a tool those same clients use to underwrite, and (c) the recipient of referral fees on deals he steers elsewhere. Steering a lead to a referral partner for a fee while presenting as a 'neutral' underwriting terminal is a textbook undisclosed-conflict / steering exposure under TRESA's conflict rules — the referral fee must be disclosed to the consumer, not just papered through EXP. Second, soliciting users to upload their own transaction outcomes raises privacy/consent and potentially TRREB-data-commingling questions if any uploaded field is then cross-referenced against VOW records (the plan's whole value is calibrating against the feed). The committee's 'sell competence not Switzerland' framing handles the marketing optics but not the legal duty to disclose the referral-fee conflict at the point of each representation/referral. _Fix:_ Add to the counsel scope: (a) mandatory written referral-fee-conflict disclosure to every consumer at the CTA/intake step (the same consent gate), stating the founder may receive a fee for referring them and is a licensed agent with a commercial interest — this is a per-transaction TRESA duty, not a one-time terms-of-use line; (b) a firewall ensuring user-uploaded outcome data is never joined to or re-published alongside VOW Listing Information in a way that re-exposes gated values, and that upload consent covers the analytics use; (c) confirm the 'neutral terminal' copy doesn't itself constitute a misrepresentation given the disclosed commercial interest. None of this is irreducible, but it must be designed into Phase 1's disclosure step, not bolted on.
- **Per-close and referral-net figures are ~20–35% high for THIS segment; the referral rail (the millionaire base case) takes the biggest haircut** - The $26.5K gross assumes a $1.06M average buyer-side close, but flippers deliberately buy cheaper distressed stock ($700–900K), dropping gross to ~$17.5–22.5K and net pre-cap to ~$14–18K — so the personal-close rail is ~20% high. The referral math is worse: $6.6K claimed as '25% of co-op,' but a referral is 25% of the RECEIVING agent's commission AFTER the receiving brokerage's split, and then EXP takes its cut when it flows to you — net to founder is ~$4–5K, not $6.6K. Since referral is explicitly the base case for the millionaire path, this ~25–35% haircut compounds across the 8–18 referred deals and is the single largest erosion of the High-case total. _Fix:_ Re-run the Section-4 table with a segment-specific $800K average close (gross ~$18–20K, net pre-cap ~$15K) and a referral NET of $4–5K after both the receiving split and EXP's cut. The Base case lands ~$170–185K and High ~$300–350K (not $480K). State the corrected floor as the real base case and reserve 'millionaire' strictly for the multi-year compounding High scenario, flagged as conditional on conversion ≥4% holding.
- **At $0 CAC, the time-cost of acquisition is the hidden CAC — and it directly competes with the same scarce hours that produce the only high-margin revenue** - The plan correctly carves recruiting out of build hours, but the unit economics never price the founder's hour. Warm-community seeding, deal servicing, and feed-outage SRE all draw from the SAME finite nights-and-weekends pool; every hour spent acquiring or babysitting the feed is an hour NOT servicing a $15–21K close. With a hard ~8-close calendar cap, the binding constraint isn't leads or dollars-per-deal — it's that acquisition, servicing, and ops are rivalrous claims on one person's evenings. This means the referral rail isn't truly 'zero founder time': sourcing and qualifying a referable lead still consumes the scarce acquisition hour, so 'scale through other people's hours' understates the founder-hour cost of originating each referral. _Fix:_ Add an explicit hours-budget to the model: estimate weekly available founder-hours, allocate them across acquisition / servicing / ops, and show that the Base/High deal counts FIT within that budget (they likely don't at High). Treat the founder-hour as the true CAC denominator and compute revenue-per-founder-hour for each rail — this will likely reveal that beyond ~8 personal closes, ONLY pure-async referral (already-warm inbound) scales, hard-capping originated-referral volume well below the 18 in the High case.
