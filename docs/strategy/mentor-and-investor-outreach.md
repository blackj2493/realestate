# Mentor, Advisor & Capital Outreach Playbook

**Question this answers:** as a solo founder, who do I connect with to grow PureProperty —
mentors, advisors, and investors — and in what order?

> ## ⛔ Sequencing gate — read before contacting any investor
>
> `2026-06-08-beat-housesigma-business-strategy.md` §4 states the honest base case in the
> founder's own words: *"a $150–200K/yr lifestyle origination business with a copyable
> front-end — high income, **not venture-defensible**."* Appendix A carries two **CRITICAL**
> unresolved holes that any competent diligence finds in week one:
>
> 1. **The BoR authorization is unasked.** Whether eXp's Broker-of-Record will put their
>    registration behind a public, subscription-gated analytics brand under their VOW —
>    not *how* remuneration flows, but *whether the licence extends to this at all*.
> 2. **The remuneration structure is asserted, not validated.** Under TRESA a salesperson
>    can only be paid for trade-related services by their brokerage.
>
> **Do not pitch equity investors before both close.** Not because the pitch would fail —
> because a "no" from the BoR after money is in is materially worse than a "no" before,
> and because outside equity in a trade-connected revenue stream is *itself* a question
> for the same counsel opinion, not a settled matter. Introducing a shareholder who is
> paid out of commission income may widen the exact exposure Appendix A flags.
>
> **Mentors and non-dilutive money have no such gate. Start there today.**

## The core reframe

You asked how to find people to "invest in my business to take it to the next level."
Read against your own operating plan, that is probably the wrong instrument:

- **Institutional VCs fund software that scales without the founder.** Your plan's base
  case scales through *your* licence, *your* calendar, and *your* micro-market
  relationships (Moat #1) — the plan is explicit that relationship depth is the only
  moat capital cannot erode. That is a strength as a business and a disqualifier as a
  venture asset. A VC reading §4 sees a ~$315K founder take-home ceiling, not a fund
  return.
- **What you actually lack is not capital.** Your build velocity is *abundant* (50
  commits/month). Money buys engineering. You are not engineering-constrained.
- **What you actually lack is weekday human hours, distribution, and two legal
  signatures.** None of those are bought with a seed round. All three are unlocked by
  the right *people*.

So: **recruit for the constraint, not for the cash.** The ranking below is by
leverage-per-hour against that constraint.

## Tier 1 — Operator mentors (the ones who match your actual business)

Your business is a **brokerage origination funnel with a software wedge**. The people who
have solved your specific problem are not tech founders. They are:

**a) Top-producing GTA investor-focused agents and team leads.**
Someone doing 30–60 investor-side deals a year has already solved the exact problem your
Appendix A calls unresolved: how referral hand-offs actually get staffed on weekday hours
without killing the lead. Ask them directly — *"when a hot flipper lead comes in at 11am
Tuesday and you're unavailable, what physically happens?"* That single answer either
validates or halves your referral rail, which is your entire base case.

**b) Brokerage owners and eXp-network leaders.**
eXp's structure is unusually good for this: it runs on sponsorship trees and revenue
share, so senior agents are *structurally incentivized* to help you succeed. Mine your own
upline. Most founders in your position ignore this and go looking for strangers.

**c) Founders who built Canadian real-estate data products.**
HouseSigma, Wahi, HonestDoor, Properly, Zoocasa, Zolo, WOWA, Better Dwelling / Valery.
Several are named in `data-outreach-playbook.md` as prior art — meaning you have already
done the homework that makes an intelligent approach possible. **The people who publish
the same metrics you do are the single most under-used contact pool you have.** They are
not purely competitors; the outreach playbook already treats them as a landscape.

**d) Your customers, as an advisory council.**
Recruit **5–10 named Brampton/Mississauga flippers** as a founding advisory group. This is
the highest-value item on this page, because it does double duty: it is a mentor network
*and* it is the **Phase-2 conversion probe your plan makes mandatory** (§7: "≥5 of cohort
do either" rep-intent or $59 price-intent — the weak-stickiness kill gate). You need this
cohort regardless. Building it as an advisory board rather than a survey gets you better
people and a warmer relationship.

Where they physically are: REIN, Durham REI, Rock Star Real Estate, local REI meetups,
municipal investor Facebook groups — the same populations §2's TAM gate tells you to
count. **Counting them and recruiting them are the same errand.** Do it once.

## The unfair advantage you are not using

You already own a working outreach machine — `docs/strategy/data-outreach-playbook.md` —
built to convert nightly trackers into press citations. **Point it at a second audience.**

Journalist citations are the cheapest credibility asset a solo founder can manufacture.
"Cited in the Peterborough Examiner and the Sudbury Star" changes a cold email from a
stranger's pitch into a known quantity — for mentors, program admissions, and investors
alike. The asset is built, live, and nightly-updated. The marginal cost of a second
audience is one extra list.

Concretely: the **monthly "Ontario Market Pulse"** already scoped in that playbook (5
stats, 1 chart, first business day) should go to a second list containing proptech
investors, program directors, and operator-mentors — not just press. It is a standing,
non-needy touchpoint that demonstrates competence monthly instead of asserting it once.
Someone who has read your data for six months does not need your pitch deck.

**Same CASL rule applies.** That playbook's consent constraints are not relaxed because
the recipient is an investor rather than a journalist.

## How to actually make the ask

**For mentors — never ask "will you be my mentor."** It is an unbounded request and the
honest answer is no. Ask a *specific, bounded, flattering-to-their-expertise* question:

> Hi [Name] — I run PureProperty.ca, an Ontario housing-data terminal. I'm a licensed
> salesperson under eXp building underwriting tools for flippers.
>
> One question I can't answer from the outside, and you can from experience: when a hot
> investor lead lands at 11am on a Tuesday and you can't take it, what physically happens
> to that lead? I'm trying to work out whether a referral hand-off can be staffed by
> someone with a day job, or whether I'm fooling myself.
>
> 20 minutes, any time that suits. Happy to send our Brampton data ahead of it either way.

That converts because it (a) is answerable, (b) respects their time, (c) signals you have
done real work, (d) offers something. Mentorship then *emerges* from repeated specific
questions. The relationship is the byproduct, never the opening ask.

**For advisors — formalize once they've helped twice.** Standard practice is a written
advisor agreement, commonly **0.1–0.5% equity over 24 months with a 1-year cliff**,
scaled to involvement. Do not hand out equity before someone has demonstrably helped, and
have the same counsel review any advisor grant to a **licensed registrant** — the
remuneration questions in Appendix A apply to them too.

**For investors — lead with the constraint, not the vision.** Your credibility asset is
that you red-teamed your own plan to 46/100 and wrote the failure modes down. Sophisticated
investors reward that; it is far rarer than optimism.

## Compliance wall for any diligence process

**Never put raw IDX/VOW listing records in a data room, pitch deck, or investor email.**
Aggregates, counts, and medians only — the same standard `CLAUDE.md` §4 imposes on the
product and the TAM gate imposes on Phase 0. A data room is a disclosure to third parties
who are not licensed under the feed agreements. Screenshots of your own aggregate
trackers are fine; record-level exports are not, and no funding round is worth the feed.

Corollary: **do not let a prospective investor's technical diligence pull an extract.**
Offer a screen-share of the live product instead.
