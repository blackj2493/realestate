# Move 3 — Activation + demand test

**Goal:** prove two things no amount of analysis can settle: (1) that warm operators will *actually use* the
cockpit unprompted on a real deal (activation), and (2) that a graded number changes their decision vs. their
current gut/influencer habit (demand / preference-reversal). This is the one move that needs other people —
but only ~3–8 of them, all warm, all evening-compatible.

**Time:** ~4 weeks of light, async observation (set it up in one evening).

## Why this is load-bearing
The strategy assumes a "circle," not a "list." A list is people who signed up; a circle is people who come
back on their own when a deal is live. And it assumes flippers value a *graded number* — but flippers are a
results-and-access audience, and may buy a trusted relationship + a verdict, not data depth. Both assumptions
are unvalidated and they cap confidence. This move measures them directly and cheaply.

## Test 1 — Preference-reversal artifact (demand)
Run this with **≥3 warm operators on a REAL pending decision** (theirs or a live comp).

1. Take a real candidate property the operator is weighing.
2. Show them, side by side: **(A)** a typical influencer/agent gut-take ("this is a good flip, GTA's hot"),
   and **(B)** your graded output — relist-corrected True DOM, the 2.1% expected-sale band, carry cost, the
   reno-ROI scenario, with the submarket's graded track record attached.
3. Ask: *"Which one changes what you'd offer, and by how much?"* Capture whether the graded number **reverses
   or materially moves** their decision (offer price, walk-away, or go/no-go).

- **Pass:** ≥ majority show a real preference-reversal or a quantified change in their offer/decision.
- **Fail:** they nod at the data but their decision is unchanged → the data edge is *credibility*, not
  *decision-driving*. Pre-committed consequence: data-driven share-taking is demoted to "stretch," and the
  (c) floor rests on the irreversible graded asset + the activated circle, not on out-analyzing rivals.

## Test 2 — 4-week unprompted-usage test (activation)
Give **5–8 named operators** (from Move 2 Worksheet A) real cockpit access. Then **do not prompt them.**

- Instrument and observe over 4 weeks: who logs in **≥2×/month** OR **runs a real live deal** through it,
  *without you nudging them*.
- **Activated** = unprompted return on a live deal (not "logged in once because you asked").
- **The observed activation rate sets the (a) floor target:** `circle target = activation rate × viable well
  size`. Do not assume 100%; measure it.

- **Pass threshold (proof signal #2): ≥ ~40% activation.**
- **Fail:** < ~40% unprompted return → it's a list, not a circle. Pre-committed consequence: demote the
  product to a personal-productivity tool (it still wins *you* more of your own deals — that's real), and do
  not build any growth machinery on top of an inactive base.

## Test 3 — Evening-hour decomposition (capacity, supporting signal)
On one evening, decompose what running this actually costs you per week at steady state: cockpit support,
Scoreboard/index publication, B2B report prep, your own deal servicing. Add it up honestly.

- **Pass:** fits **~4–6 hr/week** at a sustainable cadence (monthly or quarterly index, not weekly).
- **Hard rule:** if it exceeds budget, **drop to a quarterly cadence before cutting any activity** — a
  consistent quarterly index beats an abandoned monthly one (consistency is what (a) is gated on).

## Interview script (reuse across operators, ~15 min, async OK)
1. "Walk me through how you decided your last offer price — what did you actually look at?"
2. "Where do the numbers you rely on come from today, and where have they burned you?"
3. *(Show artifact A vs B.)* "Does this change your number? By how much? Why?"
4. "Would you pay for this? At what price? Would you put a deposit down today?" *(ties to Move 2 Worksheet C)*
5. "Would you refer a paid edge like this to others in your circle — or is that handing rivals into your comp
   pool?" *(tests the intro-loop assumption; expect honest reluctance — that's data, not failure)*
6. *(Day after.)* "What do you remember about it?" *(recall test — if they can't repeat one phrase, the
   positioning isn't ownable yet.)*

## Output / decision
- Observed **activation rate** (→ sets the (a) target and proof signal #2).
- **Preference-reversal** evidence (→ confirms or demotes the data-driven share-taking thesis).
- **Weekly-hour estimate + chosen cadence** (→ confirms the engine is runnable for 18 months part-time).
- If activation ≥40% **and** preference-reversal holds → the demand half of the thesis is real; proceed.
  If both fail → the honest read is a personal-productivity tool, and you've spent ~4 weeks and zero dollars
  to learn it.
